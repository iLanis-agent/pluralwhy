PluralWhy
"1 item, 2 items" is an English habit, not a rule.
Open the app
In Russian, 21 takes the singular form but 22 takes a third one. Arabic has 6 forms. Japanese has 1. If your app builds plurals with n === 1, it is wrong in most of the world.

What it does

  • Pick one of 40 languages and a number, including decimals like 1.0 and 1.5.
  • See which plural category it needs, with example numbers for every category.
  • Counting and ordinal (1st, 2nd, 3rd) forms.
  • Copy an ICU MessageFormat skeleton listing every form a translator must supply.
  • See how often the English shortcut breaks for that language.

How far to trust it

  • The categories come from your browser's Intl.PluralRules. They matched Python Babel on 124,920 lookups across the 40 languages (counting, ordinal, and one-decimal numbers) and on every category set. Both draw on Unicode CLDR data, so this checks the wiring and version agreement, not the CLDR rules themselves. It tells you the category, not the translated word.
